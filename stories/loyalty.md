## Three

The Robinson Dairy Farm sat three miles outside town on County Road 14. Tommy pulled up to the property at noon exactly. The main house was a hundred yards off the road, white paint peeling, surrounded by dead grass and rusted farm equipment. Beyond it, the barn and the old bunkhouse where the Wyeths lived.

A blue Dodge pickup sat in front of the bunkhouse. Smoke rose from a metal chimney pipe. As Tommy got out of his truck, the bunkhouse door opened and Rob Wyeth stepped out.

Rob was the middle brother. Thirty-two, solid build, with a goatee and tattoos running up both arms. He wore a stained t-shirt despite the cold.

"Tommy Brennan," Rob called out. "Didn't think you made house calls."

"Wanted to talk business," Tommy said.

"Business." Rob grinned. "Come on in, then."

The bunkhouse was a mess. Empty beer cans on every surface, dirty dishes in the sink, the smell of cigarettes and something chemical. Rob gestured to a ratty couch. Tommy remained standing.

"Suit yourself." Rob opened a beer from the fridge. "So what's this about?"

"Meyer Culhaney. You hold his debt."

"That's right. Thirty-five thousand. Plus interest." Rob took a drink. "He tell you that?"

"His wife did."

Rob's grin widened. "Veronica. Now that's a woman. I see her in town sometimes with that kid of hers. Pretty thing."

Tommy's expression didn't change. "I'm here to settle the debt."

"Why would you do that?"

"Because Meyer's a decent guy who made a mistake. And because I don't want trouble in my town."

"Your town." Rob laughed. "That's funny. I didn't know you owned the whole place, Tommy."

"I'm offering to pay you. Full amount plus twenty percent. Today."

Rob set down his beer. "That's a generous offer. But see, the thing is, I don't want to sell."

"Why not?"

"Because I like having Meyer by the balls. I like knowing he wakes up every morning wondering if this is the day we come for him." Rob leaned against the counter. "And honestly? I like the idea of him knowing we could come for his family if we wanted to."

"You've threatened them."

"I've made observations. There's a difference."

"Not to me."

Rob studied him. "You know what I think? I think this ain't about Meyer at all. I think this is about his wife. Heard you two used to be a thing back in the day."

Tommy said nothing.

"Playing the white knight, are you? Trying to swoop in and save the day?" Rob's smile widened. "That's sweet. Really. But it doesn't change anything."

"I'm offering you good money to walk away."

"And I'm telling you I'm not interested." Rob pushed off the counter. "See, Tommy, you've got your businesses and your influence. But out here? This is my place. And I don't give a shit about your money or your reputation or anything else."

"Then we don't have a deal."

"No, we don't." Rob picked up his beer again. "Tell Veronica that Meyer has until the end of the month. After that, we start collecting different ways. Maybe we start with a finger. Maybe we start with something else. Maybe we pay a visit to that kid's school. See how scared a twelve-year-old gets when three grown men pull up in a truck."

The words hung in the air.

Tommy looked at Rob Wyeth for a long moment. His face was calm. Expressionless.

"You threaten children?" Tommy asked.

"I'm just explaining the situation."

"No. You're threatening a child. I want you to say it to my face. You're telling me that you would hurt a twelve-year-old boy."

Rob's grin returned. "If that's what it takes to get our money? Yeah. I would. And my brothers would help."

Tommy nodded slowly. "Okay."

"Okay?" Rob laughed. "That's it? You're just going to leave?"

"That's it." Tommy walked to the door. Stopped with his hand on the knob. "One more question. All three of you feel the same way? About the kid?"

"What?"

"Your brothers. Jason and Nate. They both agree with you on this?"

Rob's eyes narrowed. "Why?"

"Just want to be clear about who I'm dealing with."

"Yeah. We're all in agreement. The Wyeth brothers don't fuck around, Tommy. You should know that."

"I do now." Tommy opened the door. "Thanks for your time."

He walked back to his truck without looking back. Got in, started the engine, and drove away.

In his rearview mirror, he saw Rob Wyeth standing in the doorway of the bunkhouse, watching him leave.

Tommy drove back to town in silence. His hands were steady on the wheel. His heartbeat was calm. He felt the same way he'd felt seventeen years ago in that parking lot after the last punch landed on Ray Decker's face.

He felt certain.

When he got back to the market, Jeff was in the office.

"How'd it go?" Jeff asked.

"About how we expected."

"He wouldn't sell?"

"No. And he made it clear what would happen if Meyer doesn't pay." Tommy sat down at his desk. "He threatened Jacob specifically. Said they'd go to his school. Said all three brothers were in agreement."

Jeff was quiet for a moment. "So that's it then."

"That's it."

"When?"

"Tomorrow night. The widow Robinson goes to her sister's in Bloomington every Wednesday. Stays overnight. The Wyeths will be alone."

"You're sure about this?"

Tommy looked at him. "They threatened to hurt a child, Jeff. At his school. This isn't about Ronnie anymore. These guys don't run this town. You think I can let that stand?"

"No," Jeff said quietly. "I don't."

"Then call Tony. Tell him we need him tomorrow night. Eleven o'clock. Tell him to bring the tools."

Jeff nodded. "He'll be there."

Tommy pulled out his phone and typed a text to Veronica: "Talked to them. Working on it. Don't worry."

He stared at the message for a moment before hitting send.

Then he deleted Rob Wyeth's number from his phone.

He wouldn't need it anymore.

He thought about loyalty. About what it meant and what it cost.

Then he closed his eyes and slept.

When he woke the next morning, the sheriff's department had already found the bodies.

## Six

Veronica came to the market two days later.

Tommy was in the office when Jeff knocked on the doorframe. "She's here."

Tommy looked up. "Alone?"

"Yeah."

"Let her in."

Jeff left. A moment later Veronica appeared in the doorway. She looked tired. Scared. Relieved. All at once.

"Hi," she said.

"Hi."

She came in and closed the door behind her. Stood there with her arms crossed, like she was holding herself together.

"The Wyeth brothers are dead," she said.

"I heard."

"Sheriff's saying it was a drug deal gone wrong. That they killed each other over money or territory or something."

Tommy didn't say anything.

"Meyer came home yesterday saying the debt was gone. That nobody was coming for him anymore. He didn't understand why." Her voice was steady but quiet. "But I understood."

"Did you."

"Yes." She looked at him. "What did you do, Tommy?"

"I told you I'd take care of it."

"That's not what I asked for."

"No. You asked me to buy the debt. To settle it peacefully." Tommy leaned back in his chair. "I tried. I went out there and made them an offer. They refused. And then Rob Wyeth told me that if Meyer didn't pay by the end of the month, they'd go to Jacob's school. They'd scare him. Hurt him if they had to."

Veronica's face went pale.

"He said all three brothers agreed," Tommy continued. "He said they didn't fuck around. He wanted me to know they were serious."

"So you killed them."

"Yes."

She stared at him. "Just like that?"

"Just like that."

"Tommy—"

"They threatened your son, Ronnie. What did you think I was going to do?"

"Not that. I thought you'd—I don't know. Scare them. Threaten them back. Something that didn't—" She stopped. Took a breath. "Three people are dead."

"Three people who were nothing but a menace to this town. To my town." Tommy's voice was calm. "I'm not losing sleep over it."

"You should be. You should feel something."

"I feel fine. I feel like I did what needed to be done." He stood. "You came to me for help. I helped. Meyer's safe. Jacob's safe. You're safe. You've got control of your life back. That's what you wanted."

"I wanted you to fix the problem, not—" She gestured helplessly. "Not this."

"This is how I fix problems, Ronnie. You've always known that."

She looked away. Out the small window at the parking lot. "I know."

They stood in silence for a moment.

"I didn't ask you to kill anyone," she said quietly.

"No. You just asked me to make it go away. To use my influence. My power. To solve a problem you couldn't solve." Tommy moved closer. "But you knew what that meant. You knew who I am. What I'm capable of. You've always known."

"That's not fair."

"Isn't it? You left me seventeen years ago because I beat Ray Decker half to death for threatening you and your brother. You said you couldn't be with someone who solved problems with violence. But here you are. When you're scared, when you need someone to protect you, who do you come to?"

Veronica's jaw tightened. "That's different."

"How?"

"Because I didn't ask you to—" She stopped. "I didn't want anyone to die."

"But you're glad they did."

She looked at him sharply. "What?"

"You're glad they're dead. You're relieved. I can see it in your face." Tommy's voice was gentle. "You're horrified at what I did. But you're also grateful. Because now Jacob is safe. And Meyer is safe. And you don't have to lie awake at night wondering if the Wyeths are going to show up at your door."

Veronica's eyes filled with tears. "Don't."

"I'm not judging you. I'm just saying what's true."

"You don't get to do that. You don't get to make me complicit in this."

"I'm not making you anything. You came to me. You knew what could happen, even if you didn't want to admit it." He paused. "And now it's done. And you can go back to your life. With Meyer. With Jacob. Safe."

She wiped her eyes. "Why did you do it? Really?"

"I told you. They threatened—"

"No. I mean why did you do it for me? After everything. After I left. After I chose someone else." Her voice broke slightly. "Why would you still—"

"Because I love you," Tommy said simply. "I've always loved you Ronnie. Even after you left. Even after you married Meyer. Even knowing you'd never choose me."

The words hung in the air.

Veronica stared at him. "Tommy—"

"You chose Meyer. You chose that life. And I respect that. But when someone threatens him, when someone threatens your family, I'm going to protect them. Because that's what I do."

"Even if it means—"

"Even if it means you look at me the way you're looking at me right now." He stepped back. "I know who I am. I know what I'm capable of. And I'm not going to apologize for keeping you safe."

Veronica's shoulders shook. She covered her face with her hands.

"I should go," she said.

"Yeah."

She moved to the door. Stopped with her hand on the knob. "Thank you."

Tommy didn't say anything.

"I mean it. Thank you for—" She couldn't finish. "Our son is safe. That's all that matters."

"Yes."

She looked back at him. Her face was wet with tears. "I'm sorry. For everything. For leaving. For coming here. For asking you to—"

"Don't be sorry. You did what you had to do." Tommy's expression was sad. Resigned. "We both did."

She nodded. Opened the door.

"Ronnie," he said.

She turned.

"If you ever need anything. If Jacob ever needs anything. You come to me. Understood?"

Fresh tears spilled down her face. "I can't. You know I can't."

"I know. But the offer stands."

She left without saying anything else.

Tommy stood in the office listening to the bell chime as the front door closed. Then he sat down at his desk and went back to work.

Jeff came in a few minutes later. "She gone?"

"Yeah."

"How'd she take it?"

"About how you'd expect."

"She coming back?"

"No." Tommy looked at the invoices on his desk. "But it doesn't matter. She's safe. That's enough."

Jeff watched him for a long moment. Then he nodded and left.

Tommy worked through the afternoon. Customers came and went. The phone rang. Life continued.

That evening he stood at the bay window of his house and looked out at Main Street. The lights were coming on. People walking to dinner. Cars passing by. Normal life in a small town where three men had been killed two nights ago and nobody was mourning them.

He thought about Veronica's face when she realized what he'd done. The horror and the relief mixing together. The way she'd thanked him even as she cried.

He thought about Jacob. Twelve years old. Safe now. Playing basketball. Living a life that would never include Tommy.

He thought about loyalty. About what it meant to protect people even when they couldn't accept you for it.

The phone in his pocket buzzed. A text from Veronica. Just two words:

"Thank you."

Tommy stared at the message for a long time. Then he typed back:

"Always."

He knew she wouldn't respond. Knew he might not hear from her again for years, if ever. But that was okay. She was safe. Jacob was safe. That was what mattered.

Tommy put his phone away and went downstairs to the bar. Jeff was already there, setting up for the evening crowd.

"You good?" Jeff asked.

"Yeah," Tommy said. "I'm good."

And he was. Because some things were worth the cost. Some people were worth protecting. Even if they could never be yours.

That was what loyalty meant.

That was who Tommy Brennan was.

And he wouldn't change it for anything.
