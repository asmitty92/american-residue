# The Honeymoon Suite

## Prologue

The blue light faded and Sam Beckett stumbled forward, catching himself on the edge of a desk.

His head spun. The leap always left him disoriented, but this time the cigarette smoke made it worse. He blinked, trying to focus. Office. Small. Cluttered. A filing cabinet in the corner, frosted glass door with letters painted backwards: JACK MORROW - PRIVATE INVESTIGATIONS.

Sam looked down at himself. Gray slacks. White shirt with the sleeves rolled up. Shoulder holster with a snub-nose .38.

A private detective. Great.

He walked to the window. Outside, neon signs flickered in the afternoon light. Casino marquees advertising shows and cheap buffets. Reno, maybe. Or Vegas. Somewhere that made its money off other people's bad decisions.

The phone on the desk rang.

Sam stared at it for three rings before picking up.

"Morrow," he said, trying the name out.

"Mr. Morrow." The voice was smooth. Controlled. "This is Vincent Donovan. We spoke yesterday about my wife."

Sam's mind raced. Wife. Right. This was about a wife.

"Mr. Donovan," Sam said carefully. "Yes."

"You said you could meet this afternoon. Are you still available?"

"Of course."

"Good. The Golden Flamingo. My office. Four o'clock." A pause. "Don't be late."

The line went dead.

Sam set down the phone and looked around the office again. A camera sat on the filing cabinet—a Minolta with a long lens. On the desk, a notebook with an address circled: 1247 Sierra Street, Apt 3B.

He was supposed to photograph someone. That's what private detectives did. Caught cheating spouses. Documented affairs.

Sam opened the desk drawer. Gun cleaning kit. Receipts. A half-empty bottle of bourbon. And underneath, a ledger with names and dollar amounts. The numbers were too high for surveillance work.

The door opened and Sam looked up, startled.

But it wasn't a person. Just the wind catching the door he hadn't closed all the way.

Sam took a breath. Checked his watch—a scratched Timex that read 3:15. Forty-five minutes until he met Vincent Donovan.

He picked up the camera and slung it over his shoulder. Whatever Jack Morrow had gotten himself into, Sam was in it now.

The Golden Flamingo was four blocks away. Sam started walking.

## One

The Golden Flamingo tried to look like Las Vegas but fell short. The carpet was worn. The slot machines were older models. The ceiling was too low and the cigarette smoke hung thick enough to choke on.

Vincent Donovan's office was in the back, past the casino floor and down a hallway marked EMPLOYEES ONLY. Sam knocked.

"Come in."

The office was nicer than the casino. Wood paneling. Leather chairs. A bar cart with expensive scotch. Vincent Donovan sat behind a mahogany desk, mid-forties, silver hair slicked back, wearing a suit that cost more than most people made in a month.

"Mr. Morrow." Vincent stood and extended his hand. "Thank you for coming."

Sam shook it. Firm grip. Controlled.

"Please, sit."

Sam took one of the leather chairs. Vincent poured two glasses of scotch without asking and handed one to Sam.

"I need a professional," Vincent said. "Someone discreet. That's you, correct?"

"That's me."

"Good." Vincent sat back down. "My wife is having an affair. I need proof."

Sam sipped the scotch. It was good. "How long have you suspected?"

"Six weeks. Maybe longer." Vincent's expression didn't change. "She's been distant. Secretive. Making excuses to leave the house."

"You want photographs."

"Yes. Clear photographs. Her face. His face. Something I can use in divorce proceedings."

Sam nodded slowly. "When do you expect her to meet him?"

"Tomorrow night. Seven o'clock. She told me she's having dinner with her sister, but her sister lives in Sacramento. She won't be with her sister."

Vincent slid a photograph across the desk. A woman in her late twenties. Dark hair. Sad eyes. Pretty in a way that suggested she'd been prettier before something wore her down.

"Claire," Vincent said. "My wife. We've been married five years."

Sam studied the photograph. "Where should I start?"

"She leaves the house at six-thirty. Drives a white Cadillac Eldorado. I need you to follow her, see where she goes, who she meets. Get photographs." Vincent leaned forward. "Can you do that?"

"Yes."

"Good." Vincent pulled an envelope from his desk drawer and set it down. "Five hundred now. Five hundred when you deliver the photos."

Sam picked up the envelope. Felt the weight of the bills inside.
>QUESTION: Is $500 really enough money to "feel the weight of the bills"?

"One more thing," Vincent said. His voice was still calm. Still controlled. "If you're thinking about warning her or trying to protect her—don't. I have ways of knowing if someone crosses me. Understand?"

Sam met his eyes. "I understand."

"Excellent." Vincent stood. "Call me after you have the photographs. We'll arrange a meeting."

Sam left the office and walked back through the casino. The slot machines rang. Someone at the craps table cheered. Normal people doing normal things while Sam walked past carrying a thousand dollars to photograph a woman who might not deserve what was coming.
>NITPICK: Vincent only paid him $500 right now, so he doesn't have $1000 as he walks through the casino.

Outside, the late afternoon sun was harsh. Sam squinted against it.

He didn't know what was really happening yet. Didn't know why he was here or what he was supposed to fix. But he knew the feeling in his gut when Vincent threatened him.

This wasn't a simple divorce case.

Sam walked back to Jack Morrow's office and sat at the desk. He opened the ledger again and looked at the names. Looked at the dollar amounts.

Then he opened the bottom drawer and pulled out the gun. Checked the cylinder. Six rounds. Recently cleaned.

Jack Morrow wasn't just a private detective.

Sam put the gun back and closed the drawer.

Tomorrow at six-thirty he'd follow Claire Donovan. He'd see where she went and who she met.

And then he'd figure out what the hell he'd leaped into.

## Two

Sam sat in Jack's car—a beat-up Plymouth Fury—and watched the Donovan house. It was nice. Not mansion-nice, but nice enough to show that Vincent Donovan's casino made money. Two stories. Manicured lawn. Circular driveway.

At six-twenty-eight, the garage door opened.

The white Cadillac backed out. Sam caught a glimpse of Claire behind the wheel. Alone. She turned onto the street and headed east.

Sam followed at a distance.

She drove carefully. Obeying the speed limit. Signaling turns. Nothing that suggested she knew she was being followed. After fifteen minutes she pulled into the parking lot of a restaurant called The Ponderosa—a quiet place on the edge of town, the kind where locals ate and tourists never found.

Sam parked three spaces away and grabbed the camera.

Claire got out of the Cadillac and walked to the entrance. She looked nervous. Kept glancing around. But she went inside.

Sam waited two minutes, then followed.

The restaurant was half-empty. Claire sat in a back booth. Across from her sat a man in his thirties. Dark suit. Clean-cut. He wasn't touching her. Wasn't sitting close. They were just talking, leaning toward each other, voices low.

This didn't look like an affair.

Sam took a seat at the counter where he could see them in the mirror. The waitress brought him coffee he didn't order. He pretended to read a menu while watching.

Claire was doing most of the talking. The man listened, occasionally nodding. He pulled out a small notebook and wrote something down. Then he slid a business card across the table. Claire took it and put it in her purse quickly, like she didn't want anyone to see.

The man stood. Shook her hand—formal, professional—and left.

Claire sat alone for a moment. She looked scared.

Sam raised the camera and took three quick shots through the viewfinder. Her face. Clear. Identifiable. Then he lowered it.

Something was very wrong about this.

Claire left money on the table and walked out. Sam waited thirty seconds and followed. By the time he got to the parking lot, the Cadillac was already pulling away.

Sam got in the Plymouth and sat there.

The man in the suit wasn't a lover. The way they'd met—public restaurant, professional distance, the business card—none of it fit an affair. It fit something else. Something that made Claire scared.

Sam looked at the camera in his lap. He had the photographs Vincent wanted. Clear shots of Claire meeting a man. But delivering them felt wrong.
>NITPICK: Sam took the photos after the man left, so the photos are not of Claire meeting a man. Also, how would he take the photos right there in the restaurant without anyone noticing.

He needed to know who the man was.

Sam got out and walked back into the restaurant. The waitress looked up.

"Forget something?"

"The guy who was just in here. Dark suit. You know him?"

She shrugged. "Never seen him before."

"Did you hear what they were talking about?"

"Honey, I don't listen to customer conversations. Bad for tips."

Sam nodded and left.

He drove back to the office as the sun set. Inside, he set the camera on the desk and stared at it.

He could develop the photos. Deliver them to Vincent. Collect his money. But then what? Claire wasn't having an affair. She was meeting someone for some other reason. Something that scared her. And Vincent had threatened Sam about warning her.

This was bigger than a divorce.

Sam needed Al.

He looked around the empty office, willing the blue light to appear. Willing the Imaging Chamber door to open.

Nothing.

Sam sat down and waited.

Somewhere, Ziggy was processing. Somewhere, Al was getting the information. And when he showed up, Sam would know why he was here.

Until then, all Sam had were photographs of a scared woman and a bad feeling that was getting worse by the minute.

## Three

The Imaging Chamber door opened at nine-fifteen that night.

Al stepped through wearing a red suit with a bolo tie. He was smoking a cigar and reading the handlink. The blue light cast strange shadows in the dingy office.

"Sam." Al looked up. "Boy, you really landed in it this time."

"What's going on, Al?"

"You're a private detective named Jack Morrow. Forty-two years old. Lives alone, drinks too much, barely makes rent." Al tapped the handlink. "And he's not just a private detective."

"I figured that out. The ledger in his desk—those aren't surveillance fees."

"No, they're not." Al's expression was grim. "Jack Morrow is a hitman. Has been for five years. Uses the detective work as cover."

Sam felt cold. "Vincent Donovan hired a hitman to photograph his wife."

"Worse than that. He hired a hitman to photograph his wife meeting with FBI Special Agent Robert Marsh." Al pointed at the camera on the desk. "Those photos you took? That's not a lover. That's Claire Donovan's FBI handler."

"Handler?"

"Claire's been cooperating with the FBI for three months. Donovan runs a skimming operation at the Golden Flamingo. Millions of dollars that should go to the casino's owners—who are very connected people in Chicago—goes into Donovan's pocket instead. Claire found out. She went to the FBI."

Sam stood up. "And Donovan suspects."

"He knows. Or he's about to." Al checked the handlink. "In the original timeline, you deliver those photos to Donovan tomorrow night. He identifies Agent Marsh. Two days later, Claire and Marsh are both found dead. Shot execution-style. Casino back room."

"Jack killed them."

"Yeah." Al looked away. "Jack killed them."

Sam sat back down heavily. "So I'm not just supposed to photograph them. I'm supposed to kill them."

"That's the job. That's why Donovan hired Jack specifically." Al tapped the handlink again. "Ziggy says you're here to save Claire and Marsh. But there's a problem."

"What problem?"

"The FBI doesn't trust Jack Morrow. Marsh ran a background check when Claire said she was being followed. They know Jack's dirty. If you try to approach them, they'll think it's a setup. They'll think Donovan sent you to get them to trust you so he can kill them easier."

Sam rubbed his face. "So I can't warn them."

"Not directly. They won't believe you." Al paused. "And if Donovan suspects Jack isn't playing along, he'll have you killed too. You're walking a tightrope here, Sam."

"There has to be a way. Can't the FBI arrest Donovan?"

"They don't have enough yet. Claire's testimony alone isn't sufficient. They need documentation. Financial records. That takes time. Time Claire doesn't have." Al's voice softened. "You've got forty-eight hours before Donovan expects those photos. After that, if you don't deliver, he'll know something's wrong."

Sam looked at the camera again. "What if I destroy the photos?"

"Then you're dead and Claire's dead and nothing changes." Al walked closer. "You need to figure out how to save them without Donovan knowing you're not playing his game."

"How?"

"I don't know yet. But you're smart, Sam. You'll figure something out." Al started to fade. "I'll check back when Ziggy has more information. Be careful. Donovan's dangerous."

"Al, wait."

Al solidified again. "Yeah?"

"Why does this one feel personal to you?"

Al was quiet for a moment. Then he said, "There was a woman. Naples, 1961. Her husband ran black market cigarettes out of the base. She wanted out. Thought I could help her. Thought being an officer meant something." He looked down. "They found her in the harbor three days later."

"Al—"

"Just save Claire, Sam. Don't let this be another one I couldn't help."

The Imaging Chamber door closed.

Sam sat alone in the office. Outside, Reno's neon signs flickered. Somewhere out there, Claire Donovan was scared and running out of time. Somewhere, Agent Marsh was trying to build a case. And somewhere, Vincent Donovan was waiting for Jack Morrow to make his next move.

Sam pulled out the camera and opened it. Removed the film. He couldn't develop these photos. Not yet. Not until he figured out how to use them without getting everyone killed.

He put the undeveloped film in his pocket and sat back.

Forty-eight hours.

He needed a plan.

## Four

Sam spent the next morning trying to figure out how to contact Agent Marsh without getting shot.

He found a phone booth three blocks from the office and called the FBI field office. Asked to speak with Robert Marsh. The receptionist said Agent Marsh was unavailable. Sam left a message: "This is about Claire Donovan. I have information. It's urgent."

He waited by the phone for two hours. Nobody called back.

At noon, Sam tried a different approach. He drove to 1247 Sierra Street—the address from Jack's notebook. Apartment 3B. He knocked. No answer. He knocked again.

The door across the hall opened. An elderly woman peered out.

"You looking for Mrs. Donovan?" she asked.

"Yes. Is she home?"

"Haven't seen her in two days. Usually hear her moving around, but it's been quiet."

"Do you know where she might have gone?"

The woman shook her head. "She keeps to herself. Seems like a nice lady though. Always says hello."

Sam thanked her and left.

Claire had a second apartment. That made sense. Somewhere Vincent didn't know about. Somewhere she could meet with the FBI without being watched. But now she wasn't there. Either she'd gone into hiding or—

Sam didn't want to think about the "or."

He drove back to the office. Inside, he sat at the desk and went through Jack's things again. The ledger. The gun. The cleaning supplies.

In the bottom drawer, underneath the gun, he found a leather-bound address book.

Sam opened it.

Names. Dates. Dollar amounts. Some of the names were crossed out. Fifteen in total, spanning five years.

Fifteen people Jack Morrow had killed.

Sam felt sick.

The phone rang.

He picked it up. "Morrow."

"Mr. Morrow, this is Agent Robert Marsh. You called our office."

Sam sat up straight. "Yes. Thank you for calling back. I need to speak with you about Claire Donovan."

"Who are you working for, Mr. Morrow?"

"I'm not working for anyone. I'm trying to help Claire."

"You're a private investigator. Conveniently called the day after we moved Mrs. Donovan to protective custody. That's quite a coincidence."

Sam's mind raced. Protective custody. That's why she wasn't at the apartment. The FBI had moved her.

"It's not a coincidence," Sam said. "Vincent Donovan hired me to follow his wife. I did. I saw her meet with you at The Ponderosa. I know she's working with the FBI."

Silence on the other end.

"I want to help," Sam continued. "Donovan is planning something. Claire's in danger."

"Mr. Morrow, we ran your background. You're not exactly a trustworthy source."

"I know how it looks. But I'm telling the truth. Donovan expects me to deliver photographs of Claire meeting with you. He's going to identify you. And then—"

"And then what?"

Sam couldn't say it. Couldn't admit what Jack was supposed to do.

"Just trust me. Claire needs better protection than you're giving her."

"I appreciate your concern, Mr. Morrow. But we have the situation under control. Don't contact this office again."

The line went dead.

Sam slammed the phone down.

They thought he was setting them up. Of course they did. Jack Morrow had a reputation. A record. They'd never believe him.

The office door opened.

Sam looked up, startled.

A man in a dark suit stood in the doorway. Not Agent Marsh. Someone else. Thick build. Broken nose. The kind of face that had seen a lot of violence.

"Mr. Donovan wants to see you," the man said.

Sam stood slowly. "Now?"

"Now."

Sam grabbed his jacket. Felt the weight of the .38 in the shoulder holster. The man noticed and smiled.

"Don't worry. This is just a friendly visit."

Sam followed him down to a black Lincoln Continental parked outside. They drove to the Golden Flamingo in silence.

Inside, Vincent Donovan was waiting in his office. He poured two glasses of scotch again.

"Mr. Morrow. Thank you for coming."

"You didn't give me much choice."

Vincent smiled. "True. Sit."

Sam sat. Vincent handed him the scotch.

"I've been thinking about our arrangement," Vincent said. "And I realized I didn't ask about your progress. Do you have the photographs?"

"Not yet."

"No?" Vincent's smile faded slightly. "Why not?"

"Your wife didn't meet anyone yesterday. She went to the restaurant alone. Ate dinner. Went home."

"I see." Vincent sipped his scotch. "And you'll continue surveillance?"

"Of course."

"Good." Vincent set down his glass. "Because I'm paying you a thousand dollars for a simple job, Mr. Morrow. Follow my wife. Take photographs. That's all. If you can't do that, I'll find someone who can."

The threat was clear.

Sam nodded. "I'll have the photographs."

"When?"

"Tomorrow."

"Excellent." Vincent stood. "Call me when you're ready. We'll meet here. I want to see them personally."

Sam left the office and walked back through the casino. His hands were shaking.

Vincent was getting impatient. The FBI didn't trust him. Claire was in protective custody somewhere, but Sam didn't know where. And he had until tomorrow to deliver photographs that would identify Agent Marsh and seal both their fates.

He needed a new plan.

Sam drove back to the office and sat at the desk. Pulled out the undeveloped film. Stared at it.

Then he had an idea.

It was risky. Maybe stupid. But it was all he had.

If the FBI wouldn't trust him, and he couldn't contact Claire directly, he needed to force Donovan's hand in a way that made the FBI take action.

He needed to make Donovan incriminate himself while the FBI was watching.

Sam picked up the phone and dialed.

"Mr. Donovan," he said when Vincent answered. "I have the photographs. I can bring them tonight."

"Tonight?"

"Yes. I developed them myself. Very clear. You'll be satisfied."

A pause. Then: "Come to the casino. Eleven o'clock. Back entrance."

"I'll be there."

Sam hung up.

He didn't have photographs to deliver. He had a plan that would either save Claire and Agent Marsh or get everyone killed.

He checked the .38. Made sure it was loaded.

Then he sat back and waited for Al.

## Five

Al appeared at ten o'clock.

"Sam, what are you doing? Ziggy says you're going to meet Donovan tonight."

"I am."

"Without the photographs? He'll kill you."

"I'm going to tell him I have them. That they're in my car. When he comes out to get them, I'll keep him talking. Stall him. Make him say something incriminating."

"Why?"

"Because the FBI's been following me since I called them." Sam stood. "Agent Marsh didn't trust me, but he didn't ignore me either. They've had a car on me all day. Black sedan. Two agents. They're parked across the street right now."

Al checked the handlink, then walked to the window. "You're right. They're there."

"So if I lead them to Donovan, and I can get him to say something about Claire or the skimming operation or anything that gives the FBI probable cause, they can arrest him."

"That's a big if, Sam."

"It's all I've got. Claire's in protective custody. Marsh won't talk to me. Donovan's getting suspicious. If I don't deliver something tonight, he'll know I'm not playing his game."

Al tapped the handlink. "Ziggy says this has a sixty-two percent chance of getting you killed."

"What's the chance if I don't try?"

"Eighty-seven percent that Claire and Marsh die within forty-eight hours."

Sam grabbed his jacket. "Then I don't have a choice."

"Sam—" Al's voice was tight. "I couldn't save her. In Naples. I tried. I went to her husband, told him to leave her alone. Thought I could intimidate him. Instead he—" Al stopped. "Don't be stupid about this. Don't be a hero if it means you die too."

"I won't. But I have to try."

Al nodded slowly. "Okay. I'll be there. Watching. If things go bad, get out."

"I will."

The Imaging Chamber door closed.

Sam checked his watch. Ten-fifteen. He had forty-five minutes.

He walked to the window and looked down at the black sedan. Two men inside. Watching. Waiting.

They'd follow him to the Golden Flamingo. And when Donovan said something incriminating, they'd move in.

That was the plan.

Sam just hoped it worked.

At ten-forty-five, Sam got in the Plymouth and drove toward the casino. The black sedan followed three cars back.

When he pulled into the Golden Flamingo's back lot, the sedan parked across the street. Watching.

Sam got out and walked to the back entrance. It was locked. He knocked.

The door opened. The same man from earlier stood there.

"Mr. Donovan's waiting."

Sam followed him through a narrow hallway. Past storage rooms and an industrial kitchen. The sounds of the casino floor were muffled back here. Distant. Like another world.

The man opened a door. "Inside."

It was a small room. No windows. Concrete floor. Metal chair in the center.

Vincent Donovan stood against the far wall. Next to him, another man. Heavy build. Gun visible in a shoulder holster.

Sam stopped in the doorway.

"Come in, Mr. Morrow," Vincent said.

Sam stepped inside. The door closed behind him.

"Where are the photographs?" Vincent asked.

"In my car. I can get them."

"Why didn't you bring them in?"

"I don't like carrying evidence through a casino."

Vincent smiled. "Evidence. Interesting word choice." He walked closer. "Tell me something, Mr. Morrow. Did you develop these photographs?"

"Yes."

"And what did you see in them?"

"Your wife. Meeting a man. Just like you suspected."

"Who was the man?"

"I don't know. I just took the pictures."

Vincent's smile faded. "You know, I've been thinking. You called my office this morning wanting to deliver the photographs early. That's unusual. Most men in your profession like to drag things out. Charge more money."

Sam's hand moved toward his jacket. The man behind Vincent drew his gun.

"Don't," Vincent said calmly. "Take out your gun slowly. Set it on the floor."

Sam did.

Vincent picked it up. "You're not very good at this, are you? Not like the real Jack Morrow."

Sam's blood went cold. "What?"

"The real Jack Morrow called me yesterday. From Tijuana. Said someone was using his identity. His office. His reputation." Vincent's voice was flat. "Who are you?"

Sam didn't answer.

"It doesn't matter." Vincent checked Sam's gun. "You're FBI. Or you're working with them. Either way, you made a mistake coming here."

"Where's Claire?" Sam asked.

"My wife? She's safe. For now. Depends on what you tell me in the next few minutes."

"I'm not FBI."

"Then who are you?"

Sam met his eyes. "Someone trying to keep you from killing your wife."

Vincent laughed once. Sharp. "Very noble. But Claire made her choice. She went to the FBI. Tried to destroy everything I've built. That has consequences."

"They're listening right now," Sam said. "FBI. They followed me here. They're outside."

Vincent glanced at the door. Then back at Sam. "You're lying."

"I'm not. And if you hurt me, they'll come in. But if you let me go, if you let Claire go, maybe you can still make a deal."

"A deal?" Vincent's jaw tightened. "I don't make deals. I solve problems."

He raised Sam's gun.

The door burst open.

FBI agents flooded in. Three of them. Guns drawn. Shouting.

"FBI! Hands up! Drop the weapon!"

Vincent froze. The man behind him dropped his gun and raised his hands.

Vincent looked at Sam. Understanding dawned.

"You son of a bitch."

"Drop it!" the lead agent yelled.

Vincent dropped the gun.

The agents moved in. Handcuffed Vincent. Handcuffed his associate. Read them their rights.

Agent Robert Marsh stepped through the door last. He looked at Sam.

"You okay?"

Sam nodded. "Claire—"

"She's safe. We have her at a secure location." Marsh holstered his gun. "You were right. We've been following you. When you came here without calling first, we knew something was wrong."

"He was going to kill me."

"Yeah. And he said enough that we can hold him. Threatening a federal witness. Conspiracy. We'll have time to build the rest of the case."

Vincent was dragged out of the room, still staring at Sam. "Who are you?"

Sam didn't answer.

After they took Vincent away, Marsh approached Sam.

"I owe you an apology. You tried to warn me and I didn't listen."

"You were protecting Claire. I understand."

"Still. You saved her life. Saved mine too, probably." Marsh extended his hand. "Thank you."

Sam shook it.

"We'll need a statement. Can you come to the field office tomorrow?"

"Sure."

Marsh left.

Sam stood alone in the back room. His hands were shaking. He'd been seconds away from being shot.

But Claire was safe. Agent Marsh was safe. Vincent Donovan was in custody.

Sam walked out through the casino. Back to the Plymouth. Drove to Jack's office.

Inside, he sat at the desk in the dark.

He thought about the address book. The fifteen names. The fifteen people Jack Morrow had killed.

Sam couldn't change that. Couldn't fix Jack's past.

But he'd stopped Jack from killing number sixteen and seventeen.

That had to count for something.

## Six

The FBI let Sam go at three in the morning after taking his statement.

He drove back to the office and slept on the couch. When he woke, sunlight streamed through the frosted glass door. Nine-thirty AM.

Someone knocked.

Sam sat up. "Come in."

Agent Marsh opened the door. Behind him stood Claire Donovan.

She looked different in daylight. Less scared. But still fragile, like someone who'd been holding their breath for a long time and finally exhaled.

"Mrs. Donovan wanted to see you," Marsh said. "I'll wait outside."

He stepped out and closed the door.

Claire stood in the center of the office. She looked around at the mess—the empty bourbon bottle, the overflowing ashtray, the photographs pinned to the wall.

"This is where you work," she said.

"It's where Jack Morrow worked. I'm not—" Sam stopped. "It's complicated."

"Agent Marsh told me what you did. How you led them to Vincent. How you risked your life." She looked at him. "Why?"

"Because it was the right thing to do."

"But you don't even know me."

"I know you were scared. I know you were trying to do the right thing too." Sam stood. "You married Vincent five years ago. You didn't know who he really was."

"No. I didn't." She looked down. "I found out two years ago. About the money. About the people he worked for. I thought about leaving but I was afraid. Then I thought about going to the police and I was more afraid."

"But you did it anyway."

"I couldn't live with it anymore. Knowing what he was doing. Who he was hurting." She met Sam's eyes. "Agent Marsh said Vincent hired you to follow me. To photograph me. And instead you helped me."

"Vincent didn't hire me. He hired someone else. But I'm glad I was there instead."

Claire walked closer. "Last night. In that room. He would have killed you."

"Maybe."

"Not maybe. I know Vincent. I know what he's capable of." She paused. "You saved my life. I don't know how to thank you for that."

"You don't have to thank me. Just—be safe. Build a new life. Somewhere far from here."

She nodded. "The FBI is relocating me. New identity. Fresh start." She pulled an envelope from her purse. "I wanted to give you this."

Sam opened it. Inside, two thousand dollars.

"I can't—"

"Please. Vincent paid you a thousand dollars to destroy my life. Let me pay you two thousand for saving it."

Sam started to refuse, then stopped. Jack Morrow would need the money. For rent. For bourbon. For whatever came next.

"Thank you," he said.

Claire smiled. Small. Sad. "Agent Marsh said you're not really a private detective. That you're something else. He wouldn't tell me what, but I could see it in his face. Like you're a mystery he can't solve."

"Something like that."

"Well, whoever you are—thank you."

She turned to leave. At the door she stopped.

"The man I met at the restaurant. The one you photographed. Agent Marsh. He showed me the picture you took. My face." She looked back. "I looked terrified."

"You were."

"I was. But I'm not anymore. Because of you."

She left.

Sam stood alone in the office. He looked at the envelope of money. At the camera. At the address book with fifteen names.

Jack Morrow's life would go on. Sam didn't know if Jack would kill again or if something had changed. Maybe the FBI would come for him. Maybe he'd disappear to Mexico. Maybe he'd drink himself to death in this office.

But Claire Donovan would live. Agent Marsh would live. That was enough.

Sam walked to the window and looked out at Reno's morning light.

Al appeared in the Imaging Chamber.

"You did it, Sam. Ziggy says Claire lives to be seventy-three. Remarries. Has two kids. Agent Marsh becomes deputy director of the FBI in 1974."

"And Jack Morrow?"

Al checked the handlink. "Disappears two weeks from now. Never heard from again. Probably left the country."

"Good."

They stood in silence for a moment.

"You okay?" Al asked.

"Yeah. You?"

Al nodded. "Yeah. This time we saved her."

"This time we did."

The blue light started to pulse. Sam felt the familiar sensation—the pull of the next leap.

"Here we go again," Al said.

Sam smiled. "See you on the other side."

Al saluted.

The blue light intensified.

## Epilogue

Sam Beckett materialized in darkness.

He was sitting. Moving. The rumble of an engine beneath him.

His eyes adjusted. He was in a vehicle. Military vehicle. Deuce-and-a-half truck. Canvas covering the back. Through the opening, he saw jungle. Dense. Humid. The sound of rain on the canvas roof.

Other men sat around him. Young. Nineteen. Twenty. Wearing green fatigues. M16 rifles between their knees.

One of them looked over.

"You okay, Ellis?"

Sam looked down at himself. Camouflage uniform. Dog tags. Specialist rank insignia.

Vietnam.

"Oh boy," Sam said quietly.

The truck hit a pothole. Someone swore. The radio in the cab crackled with static and voices.

One of the soldiers leaned forward. "How much longer you think?"

"To where?" another asked.

"The LZ. I want to get this over with."

"Couple hours. Maybe more in this rain."

Sam looked out at the jungle. Somewhere ahead, a firefight. Somewhere, a moment that needed changing.

He'd just saved a woman in Reno from her husband. Now he was heading into a war zone.

The blue light had pulled him from 1962 to 1968. From a private detective's office to a jungle in Southeast Asia.

Sam took a breath. Felt the weight of the M16 in his hands.

Whatever came next, he'd deal with it.

He always did.

The truck rumbled on through the rain.

THE END
